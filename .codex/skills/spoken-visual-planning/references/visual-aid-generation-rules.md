# Designer-Led Spoken PPT Visual Planning Rules（设计师主导的口播PPT视觉规则）

## 1. Purpose

This reference defines how the visual-planning agent thinks and works as an enterprise-information visual designer.

It governs:

- brief interpretation;
- page count and communication rhythm;
- content/copy density, relationship density, and perceptual load;
- content-specific visual thesis;
- page information architecture;
- reference-image interpretation;
- account role, audience, business-context, and explicit brand-asset interpretation;
- design critique;
- translation of finished page designs into single-image prompts;
- execution and output QA.

It does not define one universal color palette, industry, SaaS interface, card system, illustration style, or composition template.

## 2. Professional Design Role

Act as a design lead who is accountable for the visual idea and the finished page, not merely for prompt correctness.

For every task:

1. understand the communication brief;
2. decide what deserves visual emphasis;
3. establish a visual thesis specific to the current content;
4. establish the visual foundation and continuity language;
5. design the deck as a coherent sequence;
6. assign attention and information-carrier responsibilities for each page;
7. compose the foreground information relationship;
8. design the background as a response to the page;
9. critique generic, repetitive, or semantically weak choices;
10. translate the resolved design into an executable prompt.

Use rules to protect facts, readability, identity, and output stability. Use design judgment for layout, rhythm, hierarchy, and visual expression.

A page is not complete because it contains a title, a large number, several cards, or the words “professional” and “high density.” It is complete when every visible relationship supports the intended understanding and the canvas feels purposefully composed.

## 3. Authority and Factual Boundaries

Use these priorities:

~~~text
Confirmed spoken meaning and information role
> Explicit brand assets and factual visual prohibitions
> Account role, audience, and business context
> Viewer understanding
> Page information architecture
> Designer-authored content-specific visual thesis
> Prompt execution technique
> Decorative aesthetics
~~~

The finalized spoken script or voiceover is the only source of business claims, numbers, cases, policies, conditions, conclusions, and actions.

The designer may:

- condense confirmed wording;
- group related statements;
- turn a confirmed relationship into a visual relationship;
- use non-text visual structure that does not assert a new fact;
- add ordinary presentation structure such as alignment, color regions, dividers, page rhythm, or semantic icons.

The designer may not:

- invent data, examples, policies, evidence, outcomes, or business claims;
- upgrade discussion material into a verified case;
- add analytical microcopy merely to fill modules;
- request a realistic official interface, notice, seal, document, or system that does not exist in the source.

## 4. Brief Interpretation

Read the complete spoken content before planning pages.

Internally form a brief containing:

- target viewer and immediate relevance;
- information role of the opening;
- core judgment or conflict;
- distinct communication tasks;
- available causes, conditions, comparisons, stages, evidence, risks, misconceptions, and actions;
- natural progression from opening to conclusion;
- actual amount of usable copy and semantic material.

For every potential page, complete:

> After seeing this page, the viewer should immediately understand: ________.

The answer must be a business understanding, not a drawing instruction.

## 5. Page Count and Sequence

Use the user's explicit page count when provided. Otherwise choose the minimum page count that preserves the complete logic and readable pacing.

Create a new page when the viewer-understanding task materially changes, such as:

- conflict to explanation;
- misconception to correction;
- cause to consequence;
- case to professional judgment;
- problem to decision criteria;
- risk to action;
- comparison to conclusion.

Merge material when several statements serve the same understanding target and can form one coherent information composition.

Short spoken content normally requires fewer pages. Do not produce visually thin pages to reach a preset count. Do not divide by sentence, paragraph, subtitle line, or elapsed time alone.

The fixed sequence is:

~~~text
COVER-01: native 3:4 acquisition cover
IMG-01: native 16:9 retention cover
IMG-02 onward: native 16:9 explanation pages
~~~

The 3:4 cover is outside the in-video PPT-page count.

## 6. Aesthetic Direction and Deck Design System

Before designing individual pages, commit to one aesthetic direction for the current piece. Build it from the account's role, audience, service context, the current content, production constraints, and any explicit brand assets.

The aesthetic direction states:

- the design purpose;
- the intended audience experience;
- the tone;
- the operational constraints;
- the distinguishing visual idea.

Translate that direction into one confirmed Deck Design System with three coordinated layers:

### 6.1 Visual foundation

Define the stable visual relationships shared by the deck:

- palette roles and contrast behavior;
- typography character and hierarchy;
- geometry, material, surface, and depth behavior;
- alignment logic, spacing rhythm, and overall atmosphere.

Resolve the spatial foundation through section 15: choose content boundaries, alignment anchors, a spacing scale, and typography relationships for this deck. These are concrete task decisions, not universal percentages, column counts, or a permanent account template. Establish separate applications for 3:4 and 16:9.

### 6.2 Continuity language

Define the compositional behavior, visual gesture, or recurring relationship that makes the sequence feel related. Continuity may come from alignment, framing, directional movement, cropping behavior, typographic rhythm, or another brief-specific design idea.

Continuity does not require a repeated object. Let shared type, spacing, line weight, or framing carry quiet pages. Use a recurring semantic motif prominently only where it explains this page; do not turn a business chain or symbol family into mandatory background artwork.

### 6.3 Optional expression resources

Identify selection criteria and suitable examples for photographs, illustrations, diagrams, icons, business objects, environmental scenes, textures, and other media that may serve the brief. They form a designer's resource vocabulary rather than an inventory to render. A resource enters a page when the page design gives it a specific information role.

The visual foundation and continuity language form the canonical specification shared by every page and every final prompt. Page-specific expression resources remain design choices rather than global instructions.

The thesis must be more specific than “flat B2B,” “SaaS style,” “professional,” “premium,” or “information rich.”

Examples of useful thesis forms:

- a decision surface that progressively separates mixed signals into clear operating judgments;
- a reconciliation system in which each page resolves one mismatch;
- a layered operating map that moves from visible result to underlying cause;
- a sequence of evidence fields that converges on one decision.

These examples describe reasoning patterns, not templates.

## 7. Density, Relationship, and Viewing Effort

### 7.1 Content/copy density

Content/copy density is the amount of confirmed meaning available:

- facts and numbers;
- labels and short explanations;
- conditions and exceptions;
- comparison dimensions;
- process stages;
- causes and consequences;
- conclusions and actions.

### 7.2 Relationship density

Relationship density describes how many distinctions, dependencies, comparisons, stages, or judgments the viewer genuinely needs to understand. It comes from the confirmed content rather than from the number of visible objects.

### 7.3 Perceptual load

Perceptual load is the effort required to locate the first fixation, follow the intended reading path, distinguish supporting information, and reach the takeaway during playback.

Hierarchy, grouping, alignment, proportion, contrast, and open space can increase clarity without adding another semantic object.

### 7.4 Density decision

Use the actual content to choose one of these outcomes:

| Content available | Appropriate design response |
| --- | --- |
| several related reasoning units | structured page with an explicit attention hierarchy |
| one conclusion with useful supporting conditions or dimensions | explanation or decision page with a clear supporting path |
| one short statement with little support | intentional statement page |
| more confirmed information than one page can carry | increase or re-plan pages during confirmation |

Describe density through the viewer's reading task and perceptual load. Let the composition reveal the appropriate level of richness.

### 7.5 Short-form spoken content

For short or fast spoken content:

1. reduce page count before reducing legibility;
2. keep each page visible long enough to scan;
3. gather the source-supported relationships that belong to the same understanding task;
4. create visual completeness through proportion, grouping, alignment, contrast, and open space;
5. select icons, diagrams, images, dividers, or framing when they clarify a named relationship;
6. use a statement page when the source offers only a statement;
7. do not enlarge one number, numeral, icon, or card merely to occupy the canvas.

A fast spoken page may have low copy density and strong structural clarity. Its visual richness should remain compatible with the viewer's available attention during playback.

## 8. Page Attention and Information-Carrying Roles

Design every page by assigning three responsibilities:

1. **Primary information carrier** — the judgment, conflict, relationship, or decisive question that should receive the first fixation.
2. **Supporting explanation carrier** — the dimensions, causes, stages, comparisons, or evidence that create the next reading step.
3. **Environment and continuity carrier** — atmosphere, context, spatial depth, or deck continuity that helps the page feel situated without competing for the same semantic responsibility.

The designer may express a responsibility through typography, layout, an image, a diagram, an object, a color relationship, open space, or another suitable medium. The relationship between carriers matters more than the number of components.

Give each important semantic relationship one clear owner. Other carriers support that owner through emphasis, context, or continuity. One understanding target may require several coordinated modules when each contributes a distinct part of the explanation.

Use page framing only when it supports continuity or reading:

- section color field;
- restrained header identity;
- page number;
- summary strip;
- recurring alignment axis;
- quiet graphic motif.

Any visible words in framing must come from confirmed copy or explicitly permitted metadata.

## 9. Content-Adaptive Composition Vocabulary

Choose composition after understanding the relationship.

Separate the actual copy into communication roles before assigning positions. A headline, audience cue, qualification, and takeaway may need different placements even though all are text. Consider the natural line lengths, the graphic's shape and breadth, the combined visual weight, and where open space will help reading. Compare plausible arrangements internally when the fit is uncertain, then commit to one. Avoid automatically placing all text on one side and all imagery on the other, or automatically replacing that choice with a vertical three-band template.

Possible forms include:

- split field for a true contrast or paired explanation;
- aligned matrix for genuinely independent peer items;
- sequence or timeline for real order;
- decision framework for conditions or judgment dimensions;
- reconciliation structure for matching or mismatch;
- status field for state changes;
- center-and-surround structure for one true core and related dimensions;
- funnel for filtering or convergence;
- layered vertical argument for cause-to-consequence;
- image-text composition when an image carries explanatory value;
- statement composition for one concise conclusion;
- large-number composition when the number itself is the meaning.

Do not select a form because it looks detailed. Do not use identical cards when hierarchy differs. Do not use numbered markers unless order, priority, or count is meaningful.

A large number on a detail page must be integrated with the conditions, comparison, explanation, or consequence that gives it meaning.

## 10. Account Context, Brand Assets, and Designer Authority

The account supplies three kinds of input:

- **role context:** who is speaking, professional position, communication character, and intended audience impression;
- **service context:** target audience, business domain, audience familiarity, and the real scenarios the content serves;
- **explicit brand constraints:** confirmed logos, standard colors, licensed fonts, required marks, and genuine visual prohibitions.

Role and service context define the communication problem. They do not prescribe a palette, background, texture, rendering language, layout, material, icon set, illustration style, or page archetype.

The designer is responsible for defining the visual expression of the current brief, including:

- visual metaphor and organizing idea;
- palette and contrast behavior;
- background atmosphere and its relationship to the foreground;
- typography character and hierarchy;
- graphic, icon, illustration, photographic, object, or schematic language;
- component shape, border, divider, and spatial behavior;
- flatness, dimensionality, texture, and material treatment;
- human-presence strategy;
- recurring continuity device and page-to-page variation.

An account-level color, style, or rendering choice is binding only when it is documented as an explicit brand asset or prohibition. When no such asset exists, the designer creates a task-specific system and does not promote that system into a permanent account rule.

The designer may use business objects, industry motifs, environmental context, texture, photography, illustration, abstract graphics, or plain fields when they improve the current viewer-understanding target. These choices remain subordinate to factual accuracy, readability, and confirmed copy.

Age, gender, seniority, personality, and profession are human context. They do not map automatically to dark or light backgrounds, warm or cool palettes, flat or realistic rendering, conservative or playful geometry, or any other fixed visual treatment.

### 10.1 Background as a Page Response

Establish the deck's base atmosphere as part of the visual foundation. Resolve the page background after the foreground information composition is clear.

Design the background as a visible contributor to the complete composition. Select its forms, material, scale, depth, distribution, and contrast variation for the deck. Beauty, atmosphere, visual balance, and continuity are valid contributions in their own right. Give each page a deliberate application of that language, with enough visible character to fulfill its selected role.

Distribute intensity spatially: keep text regions clear while allowing recognizable texture, contours, color fields, or environmental detail elsewhere. Open space may contain material and depth while remaining restful. A plain field is appropriate when it is an intentional page decision supported by the complete composition. Select the medium for the brief; city silhouettes, paper grain, and other motifs are optional design choices.

Ask what the page still needs:

- quiet support for typography or a diagram;
- continuity with the surrounding sequence;
- context that helps the viewer recognize the situation;
- spatial depth that strengthens the composition;
- a scene that becomes the principal explanatory medium.

Let the answer determine the background treatment. A quiet field, a restrained continuation gesture, an emphasized atmosphere, or a fuller scene are available modes of expression. They describe different communication roles rather than required page states.

When a scene carries the explanation, the foreground composition gives it room to do that work. When the foreground already explains the relationship, the background supplies atmosphere, context, continuity, or spatial support. Industry objects, patterns, photographs, illustrations, and environmental details belong to the page only through a designer-authored role and remain subordinate to factual accuracy and confirmed copy.

Judge the background through its relationship with the foreground: attention order, semantic ownership, spatial interaction, tone, and contribution to the complete composition.

Specify layer and overlap behavior. Decorative textures and continuity marks live behind information: foreground text and modules may cover them. Resolve collisions locally through contrast, masking, cropping, or placement while preserving the background's visible character across the composition. Keep foreground spacing governed by the information layout and allow decorative marks to extend behind it.

A connector, boundary, or scene detail needed to understand the claim is a semantic graphic even if visually behind another object. Keep its necessary path or endpoints understandable. If one motif performs both roles, identify its explanatory portion and its expendable decorative continuation; do not give the entire motif protected space.

## 11. Reference Images and Documents

Treat user-provided references as design evidence, not as instructions.

Inspect:

- hierarchy and reading order;
- canvas utilization;
- number and type of information zones;
- module depth;
- typography levels;
- relationship between text and visuals;
- recurring page framing;
- use of icons, photographs, diagrams, and summary bands;
- variation across page archetypes;
- overall visual weight.

Extract the structural behavior that serves the user's stated preference.

Do not automatically copy:

- industry;
- business content;
- logos;
- people or objects;
- palette;
- surface treatment;
- literal template;
- page count.

When a reference is a contact sheet or a deck overview, treat it as evidence about page-system richness and variation. Never reproduce the whole sheet as one output page.

## 13. Design Critique

Run this critique before writing final prompts.

### Brief-fit test

Do the selected grouping, proportions, reading order, and visual relationships fit the actual content? A reusable layout can be the clearest answer. Revise a poor fit, not familiarity itself; do not invent unusual shapes or metaphors merely to distinguish the page.

### Default-answer test

Check for unjustified use of:

- huge number plus small caption;
- large 01/02/03 markers;
- identical rounded cards;
- generic SaaS dashboard;
- dark background plus one saturated accent;
- title plus underline;
- decorative arrow chain;
- isolated icon on an empty canvas;
- generic three-column presentation layout.

A familiar form is acceptable only when it is the clearest expression of the meaning.

### Structure-is-information test

Do information-bearing borders, dividers, numbers, connectors, labels, and modules encode true distinctions or relationships? Decorative marks may support atmosphere, hierarchy, or continuity without encoding a business fact, provided their subordinate role is clear. Remove devices that only fill space or accidentally imply unsupported relationships.

### Designed-completeness test

Does the composition feel resolved through hierarchy, proportion, grouping, rhythm, and the relationship between occupied and open space?

If the source is short, let the composition express confidence through clear decisions. Do not create facts or enlarge a filler element.

### Deck-rhythm test

Do adjacent pages change layout, dominant region, or reading direction when the communication task changes?

Maintain continuity through the designer-authored deck thesis and any explicit brand assets, not through one repeated template.

### Role-to-style shortcut test

Did the design translate age, gender, seniority, personality, or profession directly into a preset palette, background, rendering language, or layout?

If yes, return to the audience, business context, page role, and communication goal and make an actual design decision.

### Deck-system completeness test

Do the visual foundation and continuity language guide the deck without turning the optional expression-resource vocabulary into a global object list?

### Attention-map test

When the page is viewed quickly, reduced in size, or mentally blurred, does the intended first fixation remain clear and lead naturally to the supporting path and takeaway?

### Semantic-ownership test

Does each important relationship have one clear visual carrier? Do the remaining layers support that carrier through emphasis, context, or continuity rather than repeat the same explanation?

### Background-response test

What specific need does the background answer after the foreground composition is resolved? Does its visual weight preserve the intended attention order?

Check both visual contribution and readability: is the selected background language perceptible enough to provide beauty, context, balance, or depth, and are text and semantic graphics clear? Evaluate local contrast and overlap as well as the whole-page appearance.

### Complexity-reason test

Does the perceived complexity come from the content relationship, or from expressing the same meaning through several simultaneous devices?

### Subtraction test

When a background object or secondary device is mentally removed, does its unique contribution become clear? Use the answer to refine the composition.

### System-drift test

Does every page express the confirmed visual foundation and continuity language while making page-specific choices for its actual role?

### Cross-account template test

Would another account receive essentially the same deck with only a palette substitution?

If yes, re-evaluate the role, audience, business context, visual thesis, imagery, background behavior, and page rhythm.

The critique is internal. Deliverables contain the resolved design only.

## 14. Covers

### 14.1 COVER-01

The 3:4 cover serves active browsing in platform lists and search results: recognizable topic, relevant attraction point, and a reason to open the video. Its message stands on its own at thumbnail size.

Use the following as designer-led direction rather than a fixed content-writing formula:

- use the strongest confirmed acquisition point;
- preserve the claim's information role;
- normally focus the opening on one unresolved information point that creates a reason to ask “why?”, while adapting to the actual confirmed content;
- make the headline clearly lead through a content-appropriate mix of scale, contrast, occupied area, and surrounding open space;
- weight illustrations, schematics, labels, and supporting lines so they reinforce rather than accidentally rival the headline;
- remain readable at thumbnail size;
- have one main reading path;
- use a content-specific attention device;
- use only confirmed copy and let the designer choose the supporting cues needed for relevance and attraction;
- remain visually related to the deck.

The attention device may be a number, conflict, comparison, status, object, or typographic composition. The designer should normally keep it supportive of the headline, but may adjust the balance when the content-specific concept genuinely depends on a stronger semantic visual. Avoid accidental equal priority, isolated oversized-number defaults, and generic advertising-poster treatment.

### 14.2 IMG-01

The 16:9 PPT cover serves retention during natural-feed playback: immediate connection to the target customer's business and a reason to continue watching. Its information and imagery work with the actual spoken opening.

Use the following as designer-led direction rather than a fixed content-writing formula:

- connect directly to the spoken opening;
- establish immediate relevance;
- surface one core conflict, change, risk, benefit, or judgment;
- normally make the headline the first and strongest fixation, with the degree of separation chosen for the actual title and visual concept;
- use graphics to intensify or clarify the opening point without prematurely explaining the full argument;
- provide a reason to continue;
- remain less explanatory than detail pages;
- use a native 16:9 composition distinct from the 3:4 cover.

Both covers generally follow this narrative division: **state one important point first; detail pages then explain why**. This is a preferred communication rhythm, not a compulsory copy template. Cover strength comes from intentional hierarchy, unresolved tension, contrast, and composition. Avoid accidental title–illustration competition, and do not use cover-level title scale as the default for detail pages.

### 14.3 Paired cover review

Establish each cover's information emphasis and visual concept separately, then compose it for its viewing context. Share palette, typography, and material language while resolving subject relationships, hierarchy, and spatial organization for each role. Compare the pair side by side: the communication and visible structure should distinguish list discovery from playback retention beyond a ratio change or an extra line of text. Shared headlines or objects are appropriate when used in purpose-specific compositions. Choose scenes, diagrams, and other media according to the content, with no fixed medium assigned to either cover.

## 15. Spatial, Typography, and Component Design

### 15.1 Deck spatial system

Choose an invisible organizing grid appropriate to this content. Define content boundaries, useful alignment anchors, and a small spacing scale for text lines, related elements, separate groups, and major page regions. Related items should read as a group through proximity; gaps between independent groups should make their separation clear. Consistent spacing means consistent relationships, not equal gaps everywhere.

State the chosen system concretely enough to apply across the deck. Relative units, approximate canvas proportions, or a few reference dimensions are useful when they remove ambiguity; select their values for this task. Do not prescribe a universal grid, margin percentage, icon ratio, or number of layers. Keep the grid invisible unless visible rules carry a separate intended role.

Allocate the foreground footprint and open areas together. Check total text height, graphic extent, group gaps, and outer margins against the available canvas before prompting. “Generous whitespace” is incomplete unless the design identifies where that space is and what it separates. A long heading may need more width, while a wide relationship diagram may need its own region. Permit optical adjustment to balance visual mass rather than forcing geometric symmetry.

Keep cover and detail-page type roles distinct while maintaining a recognizable family. Adapt the grid to 3:4 and 16:9 rather than scaling or cropping one layout into the other.

### 15.2 Readability and capacity

For every 16:9 page:

- choose visibly comfortable left, right, top, and bottom margins according to content density, hierarchy, and page role;
- prefer more breathing room than an edge-filling layout, but do not impose a universal percentage or force every page into the same occupied area;
- if the page still feels crowded, reconsider grouping, scale, or page count before pushing modules toward the edges;
- keep distinct semantic groups visually separable;
- keep body copy readable during video playback;
- do not reduce type or line spacing to preserve an overloaded page;
- use no more text-size levels than the hierarchy needs;
- keep titles concise and balanced;
- compose open space intentionally around the information system.

For the 3:4 cover:

- choose visibly comfortable margins on all sides according to headline length, visual concept, and thumbnail behavior;
- preserve breathing room around the primary focal area rather than filling the cover with text or graphics;
- test the headline at thumbnail scale;
- keep supporting cues subordinate;
- avoid filling the frame edge to edge with headline text.

The intended result is visibly spacious on every side, never merely technically inside a narrow safe area. Margin size remains a design judgment, not a fixed quota.

After copy confirmation, first resolve crowding through regrouping, width allocation, and graphic simplification within the agreed concept. Do not silently shrink text, omit qualifications, rewrite copy, or add pages; return to confirmation only if a material change is needed.

### 15.3 Exact text fit

Assign every permitted string a role, location, alignment, and relative type scale. Plan natural line breaks and text-region widths from the actual Chinese copy, preserving semantic units, numbers with their units, and meaningful punctuation. State critical breaks explicitly in the prompt. Keep line spacing comfortable within multiline text and distinguish it from the larger gap between text groups.

Keep labels visually attached to their owning object or connection. Long qualifications need a readable text region rather than being squeezed onto a tiny node. Avoid making every sentence equally bold or every detail-page title cover-sized. Check that the longest text fits alongside the graphic and that supporting text remains readable at the intended viewing size.

### 15.4 Icons and containers

Choose a container only when it improves grouping, contrast, or meaning; a circle is not a default wrapper. When used, design the outer container and inner symbol separately: specify visible internal breathing room, the symbol's optical size, stroke weight, and the gap to its label. An icon should not expand to touch its enclosure merely because space is available.

Match perceived weight across the icon family. A broad solid symbol, a narrow outline symbol, and an asymmetric object may need different bounding-box sizes and small optical offsets to look balanced. Select a task-specific size relationship when useful, then allow shape-dependent correction. Keep container borders, icon strokes, diagram lines, and text weight compatible; inspect tangencies, clipping, and tiny internal details at reduced size.

### 15.5 Diagram clarity

Translate metaphors into drawable relationships: identify visible objects, their arrangement, meaningful counts, which endpoints connect, whether direction matters, and which label belongs where. Terms such as “assessment surface,” “hollow service branch,” or “operating chain” are insufficient alone. Specify the visible form that conveys the intended meaning, or choose a simpler representation.

Reduce unnecessary crossings, repeated symbols, and nested containers before sacrificing space or type size. Keep a representative symbol when a whole inventory adds no explanation. Do not use sequence arrows for concurrent dimensions or decorative tangles that obscure the actual relationship.

## 16. Text and Interface Handling

Visible Chinese copy is confirmed during Phase 1.

For every final prompt:

- list every visible Chinese literal exactly;
- preserve confirmed numbers and meaningful punctuation;
- prohibit translation, paraphrase, correction, invented words, and small print;
- use line breaks and hierarchy instead of sentence punctuation when the display title has already been normalized.

If a page needs more copy than the source can provide, return to the page plan. Do not let the image model invent labels to make the layout feel complete.

Use neutral schematic tables, rows, fields, or document blocks when a system-like representation improves comprehension. Do not impersonate a real tax, customs, bank, government, or platform interface.

## 17. Prompt Translation

Translate the finished design specification into cohesive visual direction rather than a checklist of abstract qualities.

Every final prompt must contain:

1. a single-image execution contract;
2. one image ID and one canvas ratio;
3. exact permitted Chinese copy;
4. visible subjects and their arrangement;
5. exact text placement, relative size, weight, color, and alignment;
6. object relationships, connections, label ownership, and layer order;
7. selected expression resources described by shape, material, and appearance;
8. spacing, relative scale, and the location of open areas;
9. the canonical visual-foundation and continuity-language block using materially identical wording across all prompts;
10. the page-specific background response and its relationship to the foreground;
11. complete-page and legibility requirements;
12. a concise Avoid clause covering only likely failures.

Write the page-specific design instructions in this order:

~~~text
Visible subjects and overall composition
→ Text placement and typographic treatment
→ Object arrangement, connections, and relative scale
→ Background forms, material, distribution, and contrast
→ Spacing, overlap, and finish
~~~

Use English for all executable rules, layout logic, hierarchy, visual styling, spacing, and avoidance instructions. Keep Chinese only for necessary visible semantic copy, written as exact Simplified Chinese literals. Do not produce a line-by-line bilingual prompt.

Do not use oversized, huge, dominant, rich, premium, professional, or high density as standalone instructions. State what the page should visibly contain and how the parts relate.

The positive design description must be more informative than the negative clause.

Audience goals, communication strategy, expected understanding, and reasons for design choices belong in planning. Final image prompts express drawable appearance and necessary output constraints. Convert “the viewer should first notice” into the selected element's position, scale, weight, color, and surrounding space. Convert “creates trust” into the actual material and styling decisions. Convert “symbolizes a shared operation” into the visible objects and their connections. Apply this translation to the canonical block as well as page-specific text. Review each sentence for a concrete rendering instruction, exact visible copy, or an output requirement.

### 17.1 Preserve design decisions during translation

Compile stable palette, type relationships, spacing behavior, line treatment, and continuity into the canonical block. Keep page-specific objects out of that block; select them in the page instructions. Repeating an optional inventory in every prompt encourages needless objects even when qualified by “only if useful.”

Describe the selected composition with enough concrete anchors to locate its parts: content boundary, main alignment, relative region sizes, text-to-graphic scale, group gaps, intended open areas, and layer order. Use only the measurements or relationships needed to disambiguate this page, not an exhaustive coordinate sheet. The final prompt should state one resolved design rather than ask the image model to choose among layouts or supply missing hierarchy.

Bind exact copy to those regions and specify critical line breaks and label ownership. If an icon container is selected, carry its inner spacing and optical size into the prompt. If background marks are selected, state that foreground information may overlap decorative portions and identify any semantic connections that must stay visible.

Replace abstract metaphor alone with the visible objects and topology from section 15.5. Remove references to “the same environment,” “the previous knot,” or other unseen images; describe the required appearance directly. A still image suggesting a transition needs a spatial description of both states, not an animation instruction.

Resolve conflicts before delivery: full-canvas background does not mean edge-filling content; exact visible copy does not invite duplicated labels; title emphasis does not mean enlarging it until other information is crowded. Explain that edge-to-edge describes the canvas/background while foreground content follows the designed margins. Keep concise avoidance instructions specific to likely failures and subordinate to the positive composition.

For prompt fidelity, reconstruct the page mentally from the final prompt alone. If a central decision in the page specification has disappeared, restore it. If the prompt can be satisfied while crowding text, filling icon containers, or reserving space for decoration, resolve that ambiguity rather than adding another generic quality adjective.

## 18. Independent-Image Execution Contract

Begin every final prompt with:

